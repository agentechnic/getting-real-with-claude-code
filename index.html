Who it's for

Students, engineers, devops, managers. No cloud or infrastructure background assumed, and the anchor task is not a coding task. If you can open a terminal and you have ever drowned in a pile of feedback, you're ready.

What you'll build

A Skill you keep. You analyse a quarter of real support tickets, then harvest that whole workflow into a Skill that fires by itself next quarter.

What to bring

Claude Pro or an API key · a laptop that runs terminal commands

› nussaa  ·  3 hours  ·  mixed skill  ·  Riyadh 2026

Most people can get an AI to produce something once. Doing the same job next month without starting over is a different skill, and almost nobody teaches it.

So we do one piece of real work together, properly. Then we turn it into a Skill, send a subagent at it to find out what we forgot to write down, and fix that. Three hours. You leave with a working tool, not notes.

Why This Works

Four pieces, composed. Nobody writes a framework.

The mental model — the loop

Claude Code runs a loop. Each turn it reads your context files (CLAUDE.md, plus whatever it has opened), acts through tools, and stops for your review. The part people miss is that the reading happens every turn, not once at the start. So you steer it by editing files, not by arguing in chat. A correction typed into the conversation dies with the session. The same correction written into a file gets re-read forever.

Thesis 1

Do the work once, then keep it

Getting a good answer out of an AI is not the hard part any more. Getting the same answer next quarter, without re-explaining yourself, is. A SKILL.md is how the work survives the session.

Thesis 2

You cannot test your own instructions

You were there when you wrote them, so you read straight past the step that only makes sense if you remember the conversation. Send a subagent at it with no context and ask what it had to guess. The guesses are your missing sentences.

Thesis 3

Give it the reach the task needs

Some agents want your whole desktop. You can hand one exactly the capability a job requires instead, through a specific MCP server, and see the boundary. That is a choice most people make without noticing they made it.

The four pieces you compose
skill

Something you want the agent to know how to do. Loads itself when the task matches.

subagent

Something you delegate to a clean context. It has no memory of your session, which is exactly why it can test your Skill.

mcp server

A specific capability you choose to grant. One server, one reach, scoped on purpose.

plugin

Those things bundled so a teammate installs them with one command.

Put them together and you have a harness: work that repeats, and a way to find out when it has quietly stopped being right. Building harnesses without building them.

What You Take Home

Not the report. The thing that produces the report, every quarter after this one.

.claude/skills/ticket-themes/SKILL.md
--- description: Analyse a quarter of support tickets into a themes report with exact counts, cross-referenced against the product changelog. --- 1. Ask which quarter to analyse if the user has not said. 2. Read every ticket. Count, never sample. 3. Group by what the customer means, not the language they wrote it in. 4. A ticket raising several issues counts once, under its dominant one. 5. Check theme dates against context/changelog.md before writing. A spike that follows a release is a finding. 6. Match the format of the previous quarter's report.

You will not write that file from a blank page. You will do the job first, ask Claude to write down what you just did, then fix the two things it got wrong. A subagent will tell you which two.

After the Workshop

Forty minutes this week is the difference between a good session and a changed habit.

Lowest effort

For a folder you already work in. You feel it on the very next task, and it takes about fifteen minutes.
