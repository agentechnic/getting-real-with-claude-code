**Time:** 13:55 – 14:00
**Goal:** Everyone leaves knowing the one thing to do tonight.

## What you are taking home

Not the report. The report covers one quarter of a company that does not exist.

**You are taking home `SKILL.md`.** You harvested it from work you actually did, a subagent tried it cold and told you what was missing, you fixed it, and you ran it again. It is on your laptop and it works.

Everything else today was in service of producing that file.

## The loop, in four moves

Small enough to remember on the drive home:

1. **Do the work once**, with the agent, in a folder, with the conventions written in a file rather than argued in chat.
2. **Harvest it.** Ask for what you just did, written down as instructions.
3. **Test it cold.** Send a subagent at it with no context and ask what it had to guess.
4. **Fix what it guessed.**

Steps three and four are where the value is, and they are the two people skip.

## Tonight, pick one

The gap between a good workshop and a changed habit is about forty minutes of your own time this week. Pick one of these:

**Write one `CLAUDE.md`.** For a repo or a folder you already work in. Lowest effort, and you feel it on the very next task.

**Harvest one Skill from something you did last week.** A report you assembled, a review you ran, a document you keep rebuilding. Do the job once with Claude Code, then ask for it back as a Skill.

**Point today's Skill at your own tickets.** If you have a real pile of feedback, swap the folder and see what breaks. What breaks tells you what your Skill assumed.

## When it goes wrong at home

Three questions, in order. They cover most of it:

1. Are you in Plan Mode?
2. Did you read the plan, or approve it?
3. Is the thing annoying you actually written down anywhere?

The third one catches more than the other two combined.

## Facilitator

Five minutes, and you are probably late. Cut the go-round before you cut the takeaway.

### Run the exit poll

One question, and it decides what you build next:

> **Had you used Claude Code before today? Never / a few times / most days.**

Hands, or a slip of paper on the way out. Takes ninety seconds and answers the programming question with data rather than a guess about who showed up.

### The go-round, if you have time

Two questions, one lap, no speeches:

- What is one thing you will do differently on your next project?
- What surprised you?

If you are down to three minutes, ask only the second. The surprises tell you which beat did the work.

### What to say last

Do not end on logistics. End on the thing they came for:

> *"You did a piece of real work today and then you kept it. Next quarter that job takes you ten minutes instead of a morning, and the only reason is a file you wrote at half past one."*

### After the session

- Pin the site link and the repo in whatever group chat exists.
- Two weeks out, run a sixty-minute "show your Skill" session. People who built nothing learn from people who shipped, and you find out which parts of today survived contact with real work.
- Note which route people took to the v4.2 discovery. If the room mostly came via last quarter's report rather than the changelog, that is worth knowing before you run it again.
